These overlap, but each is best at one job.

**Start with [PageSpeed Insights](/frontend-performance/pagespeed-insights)** for
an unfamiliar page — it gives you lab and field data side by side.

**Use [WebPageTest](/frontend-performance/webpagetest)** when you know the page
is slow and need to know why.

**Do the work in [DevTools](/frontend-performance/chrome-devtools).**

## Lab and field data

Worth understanding before reading any tool's output.

**Lab data** comes from a synthetic test: one machine, one network profile, one
run. Reproducible, so it is good for catching regressions.

**Field data** comes from real users on real devices. Noisy and slow to update,
but it is the truth.

Trust field data when the two disagree. A page that scores 98 in Lighthouse and
p75 of four seconds in the field is a slow page.

## Test on a real device

The most misleading thing you can do is test on your development machine. A
recent laptop on office wifi is nothing like a three-year-old Android phone on a
congested network, and the gap is widest exactly where it hurts — JavaScript
parse and execute time.

Throttle deliberately: 4–6× CPU slowdown approximates a mid-range phone, and
"Slow 4G" is a reasonable network default.
