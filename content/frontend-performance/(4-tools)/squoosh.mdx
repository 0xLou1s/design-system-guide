> Squoosh.app

[Squoosh](https://squoosh.app/) compresses a single image in the browser, with a
split-screen comparison of the original against the result and the file size
updating live.

It is the fastest way to answer "how low can quality go before this looks bad?"
— which is a judgement call that depends on the image, and which no automated
default gets right every time.

## What it is good for

**Finding the setting.** Load a representative image, drag the quality slider,
watch the split view. Note the number where artifacts become visible, then back
off a little. Use that as your pipeline default.

**Comparing codecs.** AVIF, WebP and JPEG side by side on *your* image. The
general guidance in [image format](/frontend-performance/image-format) holds on
average, but averages are not your hero image.

**One-off assets.** A single OG image or logo that is not worth wiring into a
build step.

## What it is not

**Not a pipeline.** It processes one image at a time, by hand. Use
[sharp](https://sharp.pixelplumbing.com/), a framework image component, or an
image CDN for anything recurring — see
[compress images](/frontend-performance/compress-images).

Everything runs locally in the browser, so nothing is uploaded — fine for
unreleased assets.

There is also `@squoosh/cli` for scripting the same codecs, though sharp is
faster for bulk work.
