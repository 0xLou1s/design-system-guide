> Choose your image format appropriately

Format choice is the biggest single lever on image weight.

| Format | Use for | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AVIF** | Photographs | Smallest; ~50% under JPEG. Slower to encode |
| **WebP** | Photographs, anything | ~30% under JPEG. Universal support |
| **SVG** | Icons, logos, diagrams | Vector: scales perfectly, often tiny |
| **PNG** | Lossless needs, screenshots | Large for photos |
| **JPEG** | Fallback for photographs | Universally supported |

**Default to AVIF with a WebP fallback and JPEG last.** `<picture>` lets the
browser take the first format it understands:

```html
<picture>
  <source srcset="/hero.avif" type="image/avif" />
  <source srcset="/hero.webp" type="image/webp" />
  <img src="/hero.jpg" alt="" width="1200" height="600" />
</picture>
```

Most image CDNs and framework components negotiate this automatically from the
`Accept` header, which is less markup and less to maintain.

## GIF is obsolete

For animation, an MP4 or WebM of the same clip is routinely 10× smaller:

```html
<video autoplay muted loop playsinline src="/clip.mp4"></video>
```

## PNG versus JPEG

PNG is lossless — right for screenshots, diagrams and anything with sharp edges
or transparency. It is the wrong choice for photographs, where it can be several
times larger than a JPEG nobody could distinguish.

Use [Lighthouse](/frontend-performance/lighthouse) to find images that would
benefit from a modern format.

- [Serve images in modern formats](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/performance/uses-webp-images)
