> Minimize number of iframes

An iframe loads a whole separate document, with its own HTML, CSS, JavaScript
and network connections. A page with three embedded widgets is really four
pages, and the browser pays for all of them.

They also compete for the main thread. A heavy third-party embed — a video
player, a chat widget, a map — can block interaction on *your* page while it
boots.

Beyond performance, iframes complicate keyboard navigation and focus order, and
their content is invisible to your own analytics and to search indexing.

## When you still need one

Sandboxing untrusted content and embedding a third-party player are both
legitimate. When you do:

**Load it lazily.** This alone removes the cost for anything below the fold:

```html
<iframe src="https://example.com/player" loading="lazy" title="Product demo"></iframe>
```

**Give it a `title`.** Screen readers announce it; without one the frame is an
unlabelled region.

**Consider a facade.** Render a lightweight placeholder — a thumbnail with a play
button — and load the real embed on click. For a YouTube video this turns
roughly 1MB of player JavaScript into an image.

- [lite-youtube-embed](https://github.com/paulirish/lite-youtube-embed)
- [Third-party facades](https://web.dev/articles/third-party-facades)
