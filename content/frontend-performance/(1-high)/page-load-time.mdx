> Keep your page load time < 3 seconds

The threshold where users start abandoning. Bounce rate climbs steeply past it,
and the effect compounds on mobile.

"Load time" is vague, though, and that vagueness matters. The useful modern
framing is [Core Web Vitals](/frontend-performance/lighthouse):

- **LCP ≤ 2.5s** — when the main content appears. Closest to what "loaded"
  means to a user.
- **INP ≤ 200ms** — how fast the page responds once it is there.
- **CLS ≤ 0.1** — whether it stays still while loading.

`DOMContentLoaded` and the `load` event are poor proxies. A page can fire `load`
quickly while showing nothing useful, or paint instantly and remain unresponsive
for seconds.

## Measure it honestly

On your laptop on office wifi, almost everything is under three seconds. That
number is meaningless.

Throttle to a mid-range phone on a slow connection — 4× CPU slowdown and Slow
4G in DevTools — and measure again. Better still, look at field data from real
users, which is the only measurement that cannot flatter you.
