> Compress your images / keep the image count low

Images are usually the heaviest thing on a page, which makes this the highest-
value item on the list for most sites.

Most images on the web are compressed far too lightly, or not at all. A
photograph exported at quality 100 is typically three times the size of the same
image at quality 80, and the difference is invisible at normal viewing size.

**Start at quality 75–85** for photographs and compare. Go lower for large
background images where detail does not matter; higher only where you can
actually see artifacts.

## Automate it

Compressing by hand does not survive contact with a team. Someone will upload an
unoptimized 4MB photo, and they should not have to think about it.

- **Framework image components** — `next/image`, `nuxt/image`, Astro's `<Image>` —
  handle format, resizing and compression automatically.
- **A build step** with [sharp](https://sharp.pixelplumbing.com/) for static
  assets.
- **An image CDN** — Cloudinary, imgix, Cloudflare Images — transforming on
  request. Worth it when users upload images.

[Squoosh](https://squoosh.app/) is for finding the right setting by eye on one
image, not for the pipeline.

## Before you compress

**Is it the right size?** A 4000px image displayed at 400px wastes 99% of its
pixels. Resize first — see
[responsive images](/frontend-performance/display-size-images).

**Does it need to be an image?** Gradients, shapes, shadows and rounded corners
are cheaper in CSS. Text in an image is heavier and unreadable to screen readers
and search engines.

**Do you need it at all?** The cheapest image is the one you removed. Decorative
stock photography is often pure weight.
