---
title: Render-blocking CSS
description: Why the browser refuses to paint until stylesheets arrive.
---

The browser will not paint until it has the CSS. This is deliberate: painting
unstyled content and then restyling it would produce a flash of unstyled text,
which is worse than a brief delay.

The consequence is that **every stylesheet in `<head>` delays first paint by at
least one round trip**. On a slow connection that is the difference between a
page appearing in 800ms and in 3 seconds.

## What to do about it

**Ship less CSS.** The fastest stylesheet is a small one. See
[unused CSS](/frontend-performance/css/unused-css).

**Inline what the first screen needs**, and load the rest without blocking. See
[critical CSS](/frontend-performance/css/critical-css).

**Split by media.** A stylesheet with a `media` attribute that does not match
the current context downloads at a lower priority and does not block rendering.

```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="/main.css" />
<link rel="stylesheet" href="/print.css" media="print" />
```

**Do not inline everything.** A large `<style>` block is not cacheable and is
re-sent on every page load. Inline the critical subset only.

## The preload pattern

To load a stylesheet without blocking, request it as a preload and promote it to
a stylesheet once it arrives:

```html
<link
  rel="preload"
  href="/non-critical.css"
  as="style"
  onload="this.rel='stylesheet'"
/>
<noscript><link rel="stylesheet" href="/non-critical.css" /></noscript>
```

The `noscript` fallback matters — without it, users without JavaScript get no
styles at all.

Use this sparingly. It is for genuinely below-the-fold styles; applying it to
your main stylesheet just reintroduces the flash of unstyled content that
render-blocking exists to prevent.
