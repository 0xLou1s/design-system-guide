CSS is the material of the front of the front-end. In most teams it is also the least-owned part of the codebase — reviewed casually, learned incidentally, and blamed frequently.

A design engineer is expected to own it properly. Not "can make things look right eventually", but fluent: knowing which property causes the bug, which layout method fits, and what the browser does with each.

You likely already write CSS. This chapter is about the parts that are commonly missing, and they are the parts that remove whole categories of JavaScript.

## The baseline

Before the sections, the things worth being genuinely fluent in:

**Intrinsic sizing.** `min-content`, `max-content`, `fit-content`, and why `min-width: auto` on a flex child causes the overflow you have been fighting (see 5.3).

**The cascade and specificity.** Not to win fights with `!important`, but to structure styles so fights do not occur. Cascade layers now make this explicit.

**The box model in practice.** `box-sizing: border-box` everywhere, and knowing when margin collapses and why `gap` avoids the question.

**What triggers what.** Which properties cause layout, which cause paint, which only composite (7.3). This is the knowledge that makes performance work obvious rather than mysterious.
