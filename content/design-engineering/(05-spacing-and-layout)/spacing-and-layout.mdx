If you fix one thing about how you build interfaces, fix spacing.

More interfaces are let down by spacing than by colour, typography or layout structure — and it is the most mechanical to get right. There is a scale, there is a grouping rule, and applying both consistently will lift the quality of everything you build.

## The one rule

**Space inside a group must be smaller than space around it.**

That is it. When related things sit closer to each other than to unrelated things, structure becomes visible without a single border or background.

Most cluttered interfaces are not too dense — they are *evenly* dense. Uniform spacing gives the eye no grouping, so it finds no structure and everything reads as one undifferentiated mass.
