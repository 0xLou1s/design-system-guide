---
title: "10. Performance as Feel"
description: "A design concern that happens to be measured in milliseconds."
---

A beautiful interface that responds in 400ms feels worse than a plain one that responds in 50ms.

Users do not experience your bundle size. They experience whether the thing reacted when they touched it. That makes performance a design property, not just an engineering metric — and it is the one where your existing skills transfer most directly.

The shift is what you optimise *for*. Not the number, but the feeling: not "reduce TTI by 200ms" but "make the button feel like it responded."

## The thresholds

Human perception has fixed boundaries, and they set the targets:

```text
under 100ms    feels instant — direct manipulation
100-300ms      noticeable, still responsive
300ms-1s       clearly waiting; needs feedback
over 1s        attention drifts; needs progress
over 10s       assumed broken
```

Under 100ms, no spinner is needed. Between 300ms and 1s, feedback matters more than speed — an instant skeleton often beats shaving 100ms off the request.
