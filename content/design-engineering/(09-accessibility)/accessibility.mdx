Accessibility is not a compliance checklist bolted on at the end. It is a property of correct construction, and it sits exactly where design decisions meet implementation — which makes it design engineering work by definition.

It is also the area where you can add the most value fastest. Most teams have nobody who owns it, automated tools catch about a third, and the remaining two thirds need someone who understands both the interaction design and the code.

## The framing that helps

Accessibility is not a separate group of users. It is a set of conditions everyone passes through.

Temporary and situational impairments are the norm: a broken arm, bright sunlight, a noisy room, holding a child, a slow connection, a trackpad that has stopped working. Captions serve people in loud environments. Keyboard support serves power users. High contrast serves everyone outdoors.

Building for the permanent case produces a better interface for the common case. That is not a moral argument — it is why this work pays for itself.
