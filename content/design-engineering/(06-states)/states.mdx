A design shows one state. A component has many, and the ones nobody designed are the ones that break.

This chapter is where your engineering instincts are an advantage. You already think in terms of state machines, edge cases and failure modes — you do it for data every day. The shift is applying that same rigour to the *visual* surface, and recognising that an undesigned state is a design decision made by default.

## The practice

Build components **state-first**. Before styling the default, list every state the thing can be in, then build them all.

You will find at least one nobody considered. Found now, it is a five-minute decision. Found in production, it is a bug, a meeting and a patch.

This is the habit that most visibly separates a design engineer from a developer implementing a ticket: the ticket describes the happy path, and you ship the whole machine.
