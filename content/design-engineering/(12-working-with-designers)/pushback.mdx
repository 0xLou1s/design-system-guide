---

Not every deviation is worth raising. Spending credibility on 2px of padding means not having it for the thing that actually matters.

---

## Worth pushing on

```text
an interaction that will not work on touch
a pattern that breaks under real data
colour that fails contrast
a new component duplicating an existing one
inconsistency with no gain
anything that makes the system harder for everyone after
```

Notice the shape: these are cases where you have information the designer does not, or where the cost lands on someone who is not in the room.

---

## Usually not worth it

```text
a one-off spacing value on a marketing page
a deviation that is clearly deliberate
anything where you are substituting your preference
a detail nobody will notice and no one will maintain
```

The third is the one to watch. As your eye develops (chapter 17), you will start having opinions about things that are genuinely just opinions. Having taste does not make your taste authoritative.

---

## Ask before asserting

When something looks wrong, assume there is a reason before assuming there is not:

> Why is this card taller than the others?

The answers are often real: it holds a longer field, it is the primary action, user testing showed people missed it. The question costs nothing and is frequently more informative than the answer you assumed.

Where there genuinely was no reason, asking surfaces that too — without anyone having to be wrong about it.

---

## Separate blocking from not

When you do raise things, say which is which:

```text
blocking      missing focus state, contrast failure,
              breaks at 320px, unusable by keyboard
not blocking  2px of padding, a slightly different radius,
              a preference about the layout
```

If everything arrives as one flat list, it all gets treated as equally optional — and the accessibility issue gets deprioritised alongside your opinion about spacing.

---

## Pick your moment

The same feedback costs very different amounts depending on when it lands.

```text
early    cheap, welcome, shapes the work
middle   fine, expected
late     expensive, feels like an obstacle
after    useless, and reads as criticism
```

Five minutes looking at a design early is worth an hour of review at the end. If you are consistently finding problems late, the fix is not better reviews — it is being involved earlier, which is what the rest of this chapter is about.
