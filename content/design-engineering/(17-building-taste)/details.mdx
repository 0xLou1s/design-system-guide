> Care about details nobody asked you to care about. The `:active` state. The animation curve. The micro-interaction that makes something feel alive.

This is the habit that separates a design engineer from a developer implementing a ticket — and it is a habit, not a talent.

---

## The details nobody specifies

```text
the :active state          most interfaces have none
optical alignment          the 1px nudge (2.5)
the focus ring             styled rather than removed (6.1)
exit animation timing      faster than the entrance (7.1)
tabular figures            on anything in a column (3.4)
the empty state's words    actually useful (6.4)
loading that holds layout  no jump on arrival (10.2)
44px hit areas             on a dense toolbar (5.4)
cursor on interactive text
text selection that does not look broken
what happens on double-click
```

None appear in a mockup. None get filed as bugs. All of them are felt.

---

## Why they matter collectively

Individually, none of these changes anything. Together they are the entire difference between software that feels considered and software that feels assembled.

Users almost never articulate this. They say an app "feels nice" or "feels cheap" without being able to say why — and what they are responding to is the accumulation of a hundred small decisions that either were or were not made.

You cannot ship "feels nice" as a ticket. You can ship the hundred details.

---

## The cost, honestly

This takes time nobody budgeted. That is the real difficulty, not knowing what to do.

How to make it sustainable:

**Fold it into the build.** Adding the `:active` state while building the button costs a minute. Adding it three weeks later costs a context switch, a branch and a review.

**Fix it in the system.** A detail fixed in a shared component is fixed everywhere, forever. This is the strongest argument for doing polish work in the design system rather than per feature.

**Pick your moments.** The details on a primary action matter more than on an admin settings page nobody visits twice. Spend where it is felt.

---

## The line

There is a failure mode: spending a day on a detail nobody will notice while the actual feature is unfinished.

The discipline is knowing which details carry meaning. A focus ring is not optional — it is accessibility. A 1px icon nudge on an internal dashboard probably is.

Ask who experiences this, how often, and whether it affects their ability to do the thing. If the answer is "nobody, rarely, no," let it go.
