Taste is trained noticing: the ability to look at an interface and see *what is wrong* before you can explain why. The explanation comes later, and only if you practise.

Everything in chapters 2–5 gave you vocabulary. Vocabulary alone does not produce taste — it produces someone who can name principles while still making mediocre things. This chapter is the method that converts one into the other.

## It is specific, not general

"Good taste" is too vague to train. What you actually develop is noticing at a particular scale:

```text
spacing      4px too tight, and the group stops reading as a group
alignment    one optical edge off, on an icon that is technically centred
weight       a heading competing with the thing it introduces
timing       200ms that feels sluggish because the easing is linear
density      enough air to scan, not so much that it sprawls
```

Each is a separate sensitivity. Each is trainable on its own. That is good news — it means you can work on one at a time rather than waiting for a general sense to arrive.
