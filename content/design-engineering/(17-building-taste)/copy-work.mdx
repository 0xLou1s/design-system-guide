Paco Coursey, design engineer at Linear, previously Vercel:

> Copy and re-implement work you admire until you can proudly create for yourself.

This is the single most effective method available, and almost nobody does it consistently.

---

## The method

1. Pick one interface you think is genuinely good. Small — a single component, a card, a menu, one screen.
2. Rebuild it in code, as closely as you can, **without looking at its source**.
3. Put yours next to theirs, at the same size.
4. List every difference.
5. For each one, work out what decision produced it.

Step 5 is where the learning is. Steps 1–4 take an hour; step 5 is what changes how you see.

---

## Why it works

Reading about hierarchy teaches you the word. Rebuilding something forces you to make every decision the original designer made — and to discover the ones you did not know were decisions.

You will find things like:

```text
the padding is 14px, not 16 — it is optically balanced against the border
the heading is 15px, a size you would never have chosen
the shadow has two layers, not one
the hover transition is 80ms, much faster than you would have set
the icon is nudged 1px right of centre
the border is not grey, it is the background colour darkened
```

Every gap between your version and theirs is a decision you did not know was being made. That is the entire point.

---

## Do not look at the source

The temptation is to open DevTools immediately. Resist it until you have made your own attempt.

Looking first turns the exercise into copying values, which teaches you nothing. Attempting first forces you to *make the decisions*, then discover where your judgement differed. The discovery is only available if you committed to an answer.

After your attempt, absolutely open DevTools and compare. That is step 4.

---

## What to copy

```text
good starting points
  Linear         density, keyboard, restraint
  Stripe         typography, marketing craft
  Vercel         systems, dark mode
  Things / Craft native-feeling interaction
  Radix          accessible behaviour, API design
```

Vary it. Copying five interfaces from the same designer teaches you one person's taste rather than the underlying principles.

---

## Five times

Do this five times and you will have learned more than any article can give you — including this one.

It is slow, it feels unproductive, and it is the thing that actually works. Most people read about design instead, because reading is comfortable and copy work is not.
