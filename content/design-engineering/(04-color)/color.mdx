Colour is the part of visual design where engineers most often freeze. It feels like the most subjective territory, and picking a blue out of a colour picker offers no signal about whether it was the right blue.

There is more method here than it looks. Most of a working palette is structure — a small number of hues, each with a consistent range of lightness, mapped to jobs rather than to appearance.

## The shortcut

If you take one thing from this chapter: **most interfaces need far less colour than you think.**

A neutral scale, one accent, and three status colours will build almost any product UI. The colour in a well-designed interface is mostly greys, with saturation reserved for the few things that need attention.

When an interface feels amateurish, over-saturation is one of the most common causes — and one of the easiest to fix.
