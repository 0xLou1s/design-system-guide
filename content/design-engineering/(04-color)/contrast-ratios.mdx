The measurable part of colour, which makes it the part you can enforce.

---

## The requirements

```text
4.5:1    body text (WCAG AA)
3:1      large text — 18px+, or 14px bold
3:1      UI components: borders, focus rings, icons carrying meaning
7:1      AAA, if you are aiming higher
```

The 3:1 rule for UI components is newer and widely missed. It applies to the visual boundary of anything interactive — the border of a text input, the edge of a toggle, a focus ring.

---

## Where it commonly fails

```text
placeholder text       usually ~2.5:1
disabled states        often deliberately low
secondary grey text    the most frequent failure
borders on inputs      pale grey on white
white text on brand    depends entirely on the brand colour
```

Secondary text is the big one. It is chosen to look subtle, and subtle is exactly what fails the check.

---

## Disabled is not exempt

WCAG technically exempts disabled controls from contrast requirements. That is a compliance position, not a design one — a disabled button nobody can read is a usability problem whether or not it passes.

Aim for 3:1 on disabled states. It still reads as clearly inactive.

---

## Automate it

This is a numeric check, which means it belongs in CI rather than in review:

```text
axe DevTools         browser extension, per-page
@axe-core/playwright  in your e2e suite
eslint-plugin-jsx-a11y  catches some at authoring time
Lighthouse CI        coarse, but trend-friendly
```

Check the token pairs once, in a test that asserts every text-on-surface combination your system allows. Then it cannot regress:

```ts
expect(contrast(tokens.textPrimary, tokens.surfaceBase)).toBeGreaterThan(4.5);
expect(contrast(tokens.textSecondary, tokens.surfaceBase)).toBeGreaterThan(4.5);
```

---

## The limits of the number

Passing is a floor, not a goal. The formula is known to be imperfect — it handles light-on-dark differently from dark-on-light, and it does not account for font weight or size beyond the crude large-text threshold.

Text can pass at 4.5:1 and still be unpleasant to read at 300 weight on a bright background. The ratio catches the disasters; your eye catches the rest.

APCA, the algorithm proposed for WCAG 3, models this better. Worth knowing about; not yet the standard you will be held to.
