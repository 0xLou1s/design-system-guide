A few things worth saying plainly at the end.

---

## You will not be the best at either

The role sits between two disciplines, which means you will rarely be the strongest engineer or the strongest designer in the room.

That is the job, not a shortfall.

Your value is not being better at design than the designers or better at engineering than the engineers. It is that the thing which ships means what it was supposed to mean — and nobody holds that unless someone holds both sides at once.

If you are keeping score on either axis alone, you will always be losing. Measure yourself on the gap you close.

---

## The value is often invisible

Much of the work is prevention: the bug that never shipped, the inconsistency that never landed, the rebuild that never became necessary.

None of it shows up in a review on its own. Which means you have to make it visible — the numbers in 14.3, the reasoning in your portfolio, the habit of saying what a decision prevented.

This is not self-promotion. It is the only way work that consists of absent problems gets counted.

---

## The title is unsettled

Design engineer, UI engineer, design technologist, product engineer, UX engineer. Same work, different names, per company. Some places have no name for it and one person quietly doing it.

Read the requirements, not the title.

And as Ioana Teleanu notes, the confusion is itself informative: the role is still being shaped, which means you can shape it. That is unusual, and it will not stay true indefinitely.

---

## Why now is a reasonable time

The three forces in 1.3 have not finished playing out. Software is a commodity, design is the differentiator, and the boundary between designing and building is collapsing from both directions.

Designers are acquiring code. You are acquiring design. You start with the half that takes longer to learn, in a market where very few people have deliberately done both.

---

## The actual last thing

The design engineer is not about abandoning engineering for design. It is about refusing to accept that designing something well and building something real should be two separate acts.

Pick one interface you admire. Rebuild one component of it tomorrow, without looking at the source.

That is where this starts.
