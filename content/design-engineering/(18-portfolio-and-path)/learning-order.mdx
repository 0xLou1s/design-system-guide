## The sequence

```text
1  visual fundamentals     chapters 2-5
   hierarchy, spacing, typography, colour
   → converts "looks off" into a named cause

2  copy work               17.1
   rebuild 5 interfaces you admire, precisely
   → the actual mechanism; do not skip

3  states and edge cases   chapter 6
   → you may already be strong here

4  motion                  chapter 7
   → the most common blind spot for engineers

5  accessibility           chapter 9
   → highest value-per-hour in most teams

6  Figma fluency           chapter 13
   → enough to participate, not to design

7  systems                 chapters 14-15
   → where your background is a direct advantage
```

Steps 1 and 2 run together, and they are the ones that matter. Everything after is comparatively easy once you can see.

---

## The trap

**Thinking taste is innate.** It is trained noticing (chapter 17), and step 2 is how it is trained.

The failure pattern for engineers is reading all of chapters 2–5, feeling informed, and never doing the copy work. You end up able to discuss hierarchy while still producing mediocre interfaces — because you learned the vocabulary without the perception.

Reading is comfortable. Rebuilding something and finding out you were wrong about fifteen decisions is not. The second one works.

---

## What to build

Roughly in order of value:

**A component with every state.** One component — a button, a select — with every interaction and data state, keyboard accessible, tokenised, documented. Small, and it contains most of chapter 6 and 9.

**A small token system.** Three layers, one generated output, one theme switch. Teaches why the semantic layer exists better than reading about it.

**An interaction prototype.** One thing that has to feel right — a drag, a sheet, a transition. Iterate on a throttled device until it does.

**A migration.** Take something inconsistent and move it onto the system, with a codemod. Nothing teaches API design like changing one already in use.

---

## The single first step

If you do one thing after reading this:

Pick an interface you admire. Rebuild one component of it in code, without looking at the source. Compare. List the differences.

An afternoon, and it will tell you more about where you actually stand than the rest of this roadmap.
