For this role the portfolio is the application. Not a CV with a link — the work is the primary artefact.

---

## Build your own site from scratch

The standard advice, and it is correct: **build your portfolio site yourself, not in a template builder.** The learning is in the struggle.

It is also the first thing anyone assessing you will look at, and it is a direct demonstration of the exact skills the job requires. A design engineer whose site was assembled from a template has answered the question before the interview.

It does not need to be elaborate. It needs to be *considered* — good typography, correct spacing, working keyboard navigation, fast, nothing broken at 320px. The bar is care, not ambition.

---

## What to show

```text
3-5 pieces, deep      not twelve shallow ones
your own work         side projects count fully
the reasoning         why, not just what
the details           the states, the motion, the edge cases
```

Fewer things explained well beats a gallery. One component with every state, thoughtful API and real accessibility says more than five screenshots.

---

## Show the thinking

This is where engineers usually underperform, and it is the cheapest thing to fix.

```text
weak     "Built a design system for X"
strong   "The old buttons had 11 variants across 3 files.
          I reduced them to 3 variants and 2 modifiers,
          wrote a codemod, and migrated 400 call sites.
          Here is what I got wrong the first time."
```

Include what did not work. Anyone senior reading it recognises the shape of real work, and it signals judgement more reliably than a list of finished things.

---

## Make it interactive

You are applying for a role about building interfaces. Show interfaces, running.

```text
live demos over screenshots
the actual component, with its states
a before/after you can toggle
the motion, playing
```

A CodePen, a deployed demo, an embedded Storybook. Anything the reader can touch is worth more than a picture of it.

---

## Where side projects fit

If your day job is backend or generic frontend, side projects *are* your portfolio — and that is fine. Nobody weights them lower here, because the work is legible on its own.

This is where the loop in 16.4 pays twice: build something small, finish it, ship it publicly. It develops both halves and produces the artefact at the same time.

---

## The non-linear path

Worth knowing, if you feel under-credentialed: **no CS degree is required, and the paths in are unusually varied.**

Frank Bach studied at art school. Google's UX Engineers have come through graphic design, architecture, even MBAs.

The role is still being shaped, which means nobody can tell you that you came to it wrongly.
