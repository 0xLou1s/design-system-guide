Design engineers on marketing teams build the sites that make a company look attractive on the internet.

Think Stripe's website: hundreds of pages, rich animation, creative graphics, accessibility at scale. This goes well beyond what makes sense to do in a website builder — which is exactly why the role exists there.

---

## What the work is

```text
landing and launch pages
interactive product demos
animation and scroll-driven effects
3D and WebGL where budget allows
design system for marketing surfaces
performance across hundreds of pages
```

With higher budgets these projects call for advanced animation and creative 3D experiences — the visually hardest work in the discipline.

---

## What it demands

**Visual ambition.** This is the most design-leaning of the four. The bar is aesthetic, and "it works" is not close to sufficient.

**Animation depth.** Not just transitions (chapter 7) — scroll-driven effects, timeline sequencing, WebGL. GSAP, Framer Motion, Three.js are common.

**Performance under weight.** Marketing pages are heavy by nature and measured ruthlessly, because they affect conversion and search ranking. Everything in chapter 10 applies at higher stakes.

**Accessibility at scale.** Hundreds of pages, often built fast, frequently by several people. Systems and automation matter more than individual care.

---

## Why it suits some engineers

The feedback loop is fast and public. You ship something, it is live, people see it. Compared to product work — where a change might be behind a flag for a quarter — that is satisfying.

It is also the most portfolio-friendly. Marketing work is public by definition, which makes it the easiest to show (18.1).

---

## The trade-off

The work is often shallower in system terms. You build a launch page, it ships, and in six months it is replaced.

Some people find that liberating — no legacy, constant novelty. Others find it unsatisfying, because nothing compounds. Worth knowing which you are before optimising toward this.

Also: marketing sits closer to brand and campaign deadlines. The pace is spiky, and launches do not move.
