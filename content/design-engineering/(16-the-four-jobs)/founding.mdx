The founding design engineer executes UX, growth and brand identity as well as building the product.

Baytaş makes an argument worth taking seriously: the internet-famous software solopreneurs — Pieter Levels, Danny Postma, Marc Louvion — are essentially design engineers. They do not only build; they publish themselves, handling UX design and marketing alongside the engineering.

---

## Why this is the interesting one for you

If you can already build production software, this is the category where adding design skill has the largest multiplier.

A developer who can build but not design needs a designer to make anything people want to use, and a marketer to make anyone find it. Add the design half and the dependency disappears.

```text
engineer alone         can build it
+ design               can make it good
+ distribution         can make it found
= a product, not a repo
```

That is the whole solopreneur playbook, and the middle line is the one most engineers are missing.

---

## What it demands

Everything in this roadmap, plus:

```text
brand           a coherent identity, not just a UI
copywriting     usually more important than the interface
distribution    launch, SEO, social, community
pricing         and the judgement to charge for things
scope control   knowing what not to build
```

**Copywriting deserves emphasis.** For a small product, the words do more work than the design. A clear headline beats a beautiful landing page with a vague one.

**Scope control is the actual constraint.** Alone, your limit is attention. The skill is shipping something narrow that works rather than something broad that is nearly done.

---

## The realistic version

Most people do not start here. They arrive after a product role, where they learned to ship, saw a system up close and developed judgement about what matters.

The workable path is usually side projects while employed:

```text
1  build something small and finish it
2  ship it publicly — the finishing is the lesson
3  watch what people actually do with it
4  repeat, and get faster
```

Each cycle builds both halves at once, and produces portfolio work as a by-product (18.1).

---

## Why it is becoming more viable

The tooling collapsed the distance between an idea and a working product (1.3), and AI collapsed it further.

One person can now do what needed a small team a decade ago. That does not make it easy — distribution is as hard as it ever was — but the building constraint has genuinely moved.

For an engineer with design skills, the thing standing between you and a product is now mostly judgement and persistence rather than capability.
