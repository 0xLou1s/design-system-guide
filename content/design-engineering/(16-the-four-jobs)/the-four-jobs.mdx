"Design engineer" describes four fairly different jobs. Baytaş's breakdown is the clearest framework available, and it matters practically: the skills that make you excellent at one are only partly transferable to another.

Knowing which one you are aiming at changes what you should learn next.

## The same DNA

All four close a gap that others spend months negotiating. What differs is which gap, and what else the job demands alongside it:

```text
marketing    visual ambition, animation, performance at scale
product      systems thinking, polish, cross-team collaboration
R&D          speed, ambiguity tolerance, breadth
founding     everything, plus distribution and judgement
```

One honest note from Baytaş worth carrying: **R&D is the first thing cut when companies face distress.** It is the most exciting category and the least stable.
