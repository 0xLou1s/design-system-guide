You can already build production software. That is the half most people are missing.

This roadmap is written for engineers, which changes what needs saying. You do not need convincing that code matters, and you do not need an introduction to React. What you need is the other half: the ability to make the visual and interaction decisions yourself, rather than waiting for someone to hand them to you.

## The one-line version

Mehmet Aydın Baytaş puts it more precisely than most:

> A design engineer is **an engineer with design skills**.

Not a generalist compromise. Not "kinda good at both." A distinct discipline focused on the space where design decisions meet technical implementation.

The practical consequence, in his framing: they can take the inputs designers take and deliver the outputs engineers deliver. They do not need a designer to decide what to build.

---

## What that unlocks

Four things follow from the definition, and they are worth stating because they are the actual job:

**You stop needing a spec to start.** Given a problem, you can decide what it should look like and build it. The mockup becomes optional, not a prerequisite.

**You contribute to design work, not just consume it.** In a room with designers, you are participating in the decision, not receiving it.

**You build the things that cannot be designed elsewhere.** Interaction, motion, and feel only exist in the real material. Code is the only place they can be judged.

**You build tools that make designing faster.** Design systems, component libraries, prototypes — the infrastructure other people design on top of.

## Where this is going

The rest of the roadmap follows the order that actually works for someone coming from code: develop the eye first (chapters 2–5), then apply it to the parts of the interface engineers usually under-build (6–10), then learn to work without handoff (11–13), then the systems work that makes it scale (14–15), and finally how to position yourself (16–18).
