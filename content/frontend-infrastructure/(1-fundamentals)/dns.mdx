---
title: "1.3 DNS and Domains"
description: The highest-leverage infrastructure topic for a frontend engineer.
---

DNS is probably the most immediately useful infrastructure topic on this list.
Frontend engineers are handed custom domains, staging subdomains and "the
certificate is not working" tickets constantly, and all of them are DNS
problems wearing different hats.

The model is simple: DNS maps names to addresses, and the answers are cached
aggressively at every level.

## Who owns what

Three roles that are easy to confuse, especially when one company plays all
three:

**Registrar** — who you bought the domain from, and who bills you for it
(Namecheap, Cloudflare, GoDaddy).

**Nameservers** — the servers that hold the authoritative answers for your
domain. The registrar points at these. Changing nameservers moves the entire
zone.

**DNS provider** — who runs those nameservers and where you actually edit
records (Cloudflare, Route 53, Vercel).

When records seem to have no effect, the usual cause is editing them at a
provider your nameservers no longer point to. Check the nameservers first:

```bash
dig +short NS example.com
```

## Record types

```text
A       name → IPv4 address
AAAA    name → IPv6 address
CNAME   name → another name
TXT     arbitrary text; domain verification, SPF
MX      where mail for this domain goes
NS      which nameservers are authoritative
```

`A` and `CNAME` are the two you will use weekly. `TXT` shows up whenever a
platform asks you to prove you own the domain.

## Pointing a subdomain at a platform

You own `example.com` and want `app.example.com` served by Vercel:

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A[app.example.com]
    B[CNAME Record]
    C[cname.vercel-dns.com]
    D[Vercel Infrastructure]

    A --> B
    B --> C
    C --> D
```

The record:

```text
Type: CNAME
Name: app
Value: cname.vercel-dns.com
```

The indirection is the point. The platform can change IPs, add edge locations or
move regions, and your record keeps working because it names a name rather than
an address.

## Why the apex domain is different

`example.com` with no subdomain is the *apex* (or root, or zone apex). The DNS
spec does not allow a CNAME at the apex — it must coexist with the `NS` and `SOA`
records that live there, and CNAME cannot coexist with anything.

This is why `www.example.com` often works while `example.com` does not. The
`www` subdomain can be a CNAME; the apex cannot.

Providers work around it with non-standard record types that behave like a CNAME
but resolve to an address at query time:

```text
ALIAS      (DNSimple and others)
ANAME      (some providers)
CNAME flattening  (Cloudflare)
```

If your provider supports none of these, the apex needs an `A` record with a real
IP address — which means you are responsible for updating it if the platform's
IP changes.

## TTL and propagation

Every record carries a TTL — how many seconds resolvers may cache the answer.

```text
TTL 3600   cached for an hour
TTL 300    cached for five minutes
```

"DNS propagation" is a slightly misleading phrase. Nothing is being pushed
anywhere; you are waiting for previously cached answers to expire. A record with
a 24-hour TTL can take a day to stop being served from someone's cache, no
matter how fast you edited it.

<Callout>
Before a planned migration, lower the TTL to 300 and wait for the *old* TTL to
elapse. Then the cutover itself takes minutes instead of a day. Raise it again
afterwards — low TTLs mean more lookups.
</Callout>

## Wildcards

A wildcard record matches any subdomain that has no record of its own:

```text
*.example.com
```

matches:

```text
app.example.com
api.example.com
merchant.example.com
```

Useful for multi-tenant products where each customer gets a subdomain and you
cannot create records in advance. Note that a wildcard TLS certificate is a
separate thing you also need — see
[HTTPS and TLS](/frontend-infrastructure/https-and-tls).

## Check yourself

You should be able to answer these without looking:

- Why does `www.example.com` work but `example.com` does not?
- Why can't every record be a CNAME?
- Why does a DNS change sometimes take a day?
- What actually happens when you add a custom domain to a hosting platform?
- What does a wildcard domain match, and what does it not?

The last one has a trap: `*.example.com` does not match `example.com` itself,
and does not match `a.b.example.com` on most providers.
